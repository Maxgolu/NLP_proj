1 Stage 3: Head-Level Characterization
1.1 Question, scope and outcome
Stage 3 asks why RI-based selection and single-head intervention effects disagree, and what the
selected heads actually do in the single-hop task. Stage 2 found several large effects outside the RI
selection, small effects for several RI priorities, and definition-dependent rank correlations. These
observations do not establish copying, semantic specificity, or a writer–reader circuit.
For each head we seek an evidence-based profile:where its activity affects the answer; what it attends
to; what its contextual output writes; and which alternative explanations remain. A familiar type, a
precisely described new pattern, or an unexplained result are all acceptable outcomes. We do not
require every head to receive a categorical label.
All work uses discovery families. The 87 validation families and the location world remain untouched.
Inter-head paths, joint head interventions, circuits and faithfulness belong to Stage 4. The layer-wise
residual divergence analysis is also moved to Stage 4 as a descriptive guide, not a constraint that
rules out important computation in later layers. The proposed weight-only relation classifier is
removed: fictitious child–mother assignments are supplied by the context, not fixed associations in
the weights.

analysis. Compatible captures should share forward passes and be saved for reuse.

1.4 Planned diagnostic analyses after the core profiles
All four analyses below are included in Stage 3; they are not alternatives selected only after a
promising result. Apply them to the fixed inventory, including the random reference heads, wherever
their inputs and sites are well defined. Record the head list, input set, sites, controls and scoring
protocol before each analysis. Missing support is reported as insufficient data, never as a zero score
or a negative finding. Eligibility must not depend on obtaining the expected result.
The core profiles establish the relevant sites and interpretation. Weight-based copying and synthetic
fingerprints then test complementary static and behavioral properties; contextual RI tests the
embedding choice; attention–value interventions test routing and content causally. These analyses
may share captures and run in parallel where independent; their scientific scope is not reduced by
this scheduling.

Matched Contextual RI Comparison. Purpose and scope:compare raw and contextual
RI for every inventory head with valid saved QK events, whether or not its core profiles suggest
disagreement. Retain the extension’s metric-specific support rule (at least 20 comparisons across 10
families) for summary rankings and correlations; retain smaller samples as explicitly insufficient-

Hold event IDs, visible-token normalization, controls, anchors and family aggregation fixed. Undefined

scores on the same heads and pairs, reporting eligibility. It cannot repair missing QK events; a

not a claimed novel method.
Attention–Value Intervention. Purpose and scope:test routing, values and their interaction
for every inventory head at the final colon, including heads with small effects. Additional sites
may be selected from the causal position profile under a documented rule fixed before these
interventions; label those results as post-selection exploration. At each specified site compare
clean attention/clean values, corrupted attention/clean values, clean attention/corrupted values,
and corrupted attention/corrupted values. Use captured post-softmax attention and actual value
activations while preserving the model’s other computations. Gate the joint replacement against
the ordinary head-output patch at that site. Report both single replacements and the interaction
between them; do not force an additive or pattern/value-dominated classification.
Repeated-Sequence and Retrieval Fingerprints. Purpose and scope:test all inventory
heads on both a repeated-sequence benchmark (prefix matching and copying) and a retrieval
benchmark (attention to the copied token during defined copy events). Neither benchmark is
conditional on a prior type label. Fix their token sampling, lengths, seeds, event definitions, handling
of repeated token occurrences and scoring protocol before measurement. Run both attention and
output checks with the same reference heads. Report task success and event denominators, including
heads with insufficient eligible events. Transfer to a synthetic task supports a behavioral capability,
not proof that the same algorithm solves the kinship task. Function-vector mediation and other
additional fingerprints are not required; they need a separately justified task-level question and
protocol.
1.5 Optional extensions
Thefollowingreadoutremainsconditional. Broadspectralcopyinganalysis, function-vectormediation
and additional synthetic benchmarks may also be proposed separately when they answer a remaining
question; none replaces the four planned analyses above or is required for completion.
Controlled Representation Readout (Patchscopes-style). Trigger:a causal site is localized
but vocabulary projections cannot explain the information present there. This remains optional
within Stage 3, not automatically assigned to Stage 4. A residual-stream readout describes a
multi-component representation; it cannot be attributed to one head merely because it was captured
near that head. Compare intact and head-intervened representations in the same readout prompt,
with no-injection, irrelevant-injection and entity-swap controls. Treat decoded text as supporting
exploration, not a causal verdict; fictitious names do not rule out lexical copying or information from
the readout prompt. Questions about transfer between heads belong to Stage 4.

1.6 Reporting, completion and handoff
Average orders within family and report family support, heterogeneity and missing data. Random
controls provide context; their 95th percentile is not a universal property-detection or significance
threshold. Any uncertainty interval must name its population, resampling unit, number of draws and
quantities held fixed. Do not reuse an invalid interval or turn exploratory selection into confirmation.
Deliver a head-profile table and short evidence cards covering inventory reasons, position effects,
attention roles, contextual output, alternative explanations, and results of all four planned diagnostic
analyses. For each head and analysis, distinguish completed, insufficient support, unavailable
input and unresolved implementation; unsupported cells must not be silently omitted or zero-
filled. Track optional extensions separately (not triggered, completed, or unresolved). Completion
requires the core profiles and all four planned analyses on their applicable populations, with exclusions
documented; unavailable inputs and implementation failures remain outstanding rather than counting
as completion. It does not require favorable results or a type label for every head.
The copying, contextual-information and early/late-position hypotheses receive separate evidence
assessments, not forced yes/no verdicts. Stage 4 receives candidate components and specific
communication hypotheses to test with path and group interventions, then faithfulness and held-out
validation. Neither a similar fingerprint nor a large single-head effect establishes an edge or circuit.
