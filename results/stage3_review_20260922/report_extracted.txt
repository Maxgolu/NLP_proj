7.2 Attended-name mover classication

7.3 Matched contextual RI
Replacing the raw current-token embedding by the actual normalized value input at the same posi-
tion, with event IDs, controls, anchors and family weighting held xed (70 heads with valid events;
27 inventory heads have no saved scored event and 8 have too few matched events), does not repair
the Stage-1 statistics. On the main all-facts/all-test rst-anchor name gap ( n = 68 supported heads)
the Spearman correlation with Scope-P importance moves from −0.26 (raw) to −0.17 (contextual);
with the RI-supported family/order subset of pairs, from −0.28 to −0.19. The raw target statistic is
uncorrelated ( +0.01 raw, +0.02 contextual); the last-anchor target statistic is negative under both
( −0.31, −0.23). The embedding choice is not what makes RI fail: RI scores the current position's
own residual, whereas the heads that matter act on what they attend to, or indirectly. This is a
sensitivity analysis on a selected inventory, not an all-head result.

9 Evidence assessment of the Stage-2 hypotheses
Copying. Partly supported, with an important correction. Six heads (L27H6, L17H24, L26H31,
L18H19, L22H5, L21H6) promote the rst token of the name they attend to in ≥ 80% of events, and
L27H6 writes the answer directly ( +2.99 logits). Weight copying scores have the right sign for these
heads and for the anti-copiers L20H1, L26H23, L19H16. The Stage-2 conjecture that the negative
name-gap correlation arises because the causal heads copy names is therefore consistent with the
positive movers. But copying is neither necessary nor sucient: L18H18 (2.7 logits) and L27H6
have weight scores near zero or negative, the strongest weight copier L30H18 hurts the answer by
copying the distractor, L26H31 moves names as strongly as L27H6 but attends to the wrong ones
and has no eect, and the largest head, L17H1, does not act at the colon at all.
Contextual information. Supported for the location, not yet for the content. The heads' eects
come through their values (factorial), their attention tracks the queried fact under relocation and
query change, and their raw-embedding projections are uninformative while the contextual pro-
jections are partly informative. Contextual RI at the current position does not help, because the
information the readers use is at the attended position, written there by earlier layers (Section 4).
What that information encodes (name identity, a relation binding, or both) is a Stage-4 question.
Early vs. late positions. Supported and sharpened. The eect of L17H1, L15H25, L14H26 and
L13H10 is written inside the query-fact sentence (L17H1 at the mother tokens, is and the period;
L15H25 at the child's last token) and read later; all other strong heads act only at the colon. The
earlier layer-611 writers of Stage 2 (L6H24, L7H1, L8H15, L9H16, L12H17) are conrmed to have
zero colon eect, but their all-position eects are small ( ≤ 0.28); the important writer is L17H1, in
the same layer band as the readers.
What Stage 3 does not establish. No edge between any writer and any reader has been tested;
single-position additivity within one head says nothing about additivity across heads; the labels
name mover and negative name mover describe the sign of one head's output on its attended
token and are not mechanism claims; the changed-query control is a discovery measurement on 20
families; and all thresholds are descriptive.
